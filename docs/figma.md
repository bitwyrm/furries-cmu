# Furries@CMU — Figma design prompt

**Purpose:** Use this document as the visual and content brief for a Figma prototype. It describes the experience and art direction; it is not an implementation plan.

## Prompt

Design a responsive, multi-page website prototype for **Furries@CMU**, a campus furry club. Make it feel like a handmade web zine made by people in the club: playful, postmodern, pixel-core, retro-web, and a little strange. Aim for an indie studio's personal, illustration-led quality. Keep the site expressive and slightly risky, with a clear reading order and information that is easy to find.

Refine the existing Figma design named **“Furries@CMU Concept”**. It is the closest direction so far and most of its design should remain recognizable. Preserve the parts that already work; make targeted changes to the FAQ, add room for club photography, and give selected sections a stronger visual stance. Do not replace it with a generic new template.

The page should feel assembled by hand rather than generated from a polished corporate template. Avoid a sales-pitch tone, a product landing-page look, and a simulated desktop operating system. Make the content and original artwork the focus. Use visible pixel details, rough print marks, odd-but-considered alignments, margin notes, and varied section compositions to give it character.

Take a clear, opinionated side. Push the design toward provocative postmodern web-zine/editorial work: unexpected scale, visual friction, deliberate collisions, awkwardly bold typography, and rough print or photocopy character. Do not sand off its edges to make it broadly likable. Keep the structure readable and let the provocation come from the art direction rather than invented club claims.

### Art direction and layout

- Build three distinct destinations: **Drop In**, **Events**, and **Officers**. Keep a simple menu visible at the top, with a clear Discord join link.
- Organize each page section by section, with broad compositions on laptop screens and roughly equal outer page gutters. Keep each section visually discrete while allowing its internal layout to be asymmetrical.
- Vary section widths, alignments, scale, and image placement. Preserve a clear reading path. Avoid mirror layouts, repeated card grids, and applying one layout formula to every section.
- Use a window-like frame for only a chosen few sections, if it strengthens their content. Let the other sections read as open zine spreads.
- Separate major sections with varied, asymmetrical marks: for example, rough waves, jagged edges, broken ink strokes, or other handmade shapes. Do not use plain straight horizontal dividers, and do not repeat one divider shape throughout.
- Display titles should look pixelated or bitmap. This may come from a dedicated pixel/bitmap typeface or from custom bitmap-style lettering. Explore a specific font as a valid option and identify it in the design; do not assume the lettering must be hand-drawn. Keep body text comfortably large and easy to read.
- A little tilt, print misregistration, distortion, or overlap can add a handmade quality to display lettering and annotations. Keep longer copy stable and legible.
- Use crisp pixel shapes and occasional halftone or grid texture. Keep visual effects purposeful and leave room for quiet areas.
- Give a few selected sections an emphatic, full-bleed treatment. Each should have a solid color or pattern field, with a nonstandard divider spanning the full viewport width both above and below it. The boundaries can be jagged, wavy, torn, or otherwise irregular; vary them between sections. Keep the section itself visually distinct from the surrounding page. Do not assign a specific color to a particular section in advance.
- Reserve a broad photo-slideshow area for club and event photography. Show it as a real gallery space with image placeholders, a caption area, and visible user-operated previous/next controls or slide markers. Keep it part of the zine composition rather than a polished product carousel; do not autoplay.
- The club has its own logo. Until it is supplied, show a plainly labeled, replaceable logo placeholder. Do not invent a logo or present a decorative fox as the club mark.
- Make the design work at phone and laptop widths. At narrow widths, stack elements in a deliberate reading order, move margin notes into the flow, and keep navigation, body copy, images, and controls comfortable to use.
- Make titles and body copy visibly generous in size; avoid tiny labels and dense, cramped sections. Provide clear keyboard focus and readable contrast across text and controls.

### Section composition guide

Use this as a composition starting point, not as a reusable template. Keep the page's outside inset and reading order easy to follow, then vary the section's interior layout. Do not use the same layout for each row.

Give each section boundary an asymmetrical mark, such as a rough wave, torn edge, or broken ink stroke. Change its shape and placement between sections so the dividers feel handmade rather than repeated decoration.

### Color guidance

Use a fox-core palette. The brief calls for brick, burnt orange, orangish red, and black/dark ink, with these suggested colors available as inspiration:

`#4C251E` · `#8C0101` · `#C03415` · `#D66E48` · `#E5B992` · `#F1E4D4` · `#2468C0`

These are starting points, not a mandatory complete palette; additional colors may be explored. Keep color placement open for review. Do not treat a particular color-to-page, section, background, panel, or text assignment as decided by this brief. Present palette exploration without locking a color map into the design.

### Content and page purposes

#### Drop In

Help a first-time visitor understand the club and feel welcome joining.

- Introduce **Furries@CMU** with a large, expressive title, a logo placeholder, and a short welcome.
- Explain that the club is a safe place for furry, furry-adjacent, and curious people. State clearly that a fursuit is not required.
- Briefly explain furry fandom as an interest in anthropomorphic animal characters and the people who draw, make, write, and play with them.
- Show the four things the club makes room for: **community, food, material resources, and fursuit-making instruction**. Treat them as one related set with individual character, not four matching cards.
- Include short reassurance copy in the page itself, including **“No fursuit needed”** and **“No fursona needed”**. Explain that someone can drop in at their own pace, in everyday clothes or a partial/full fursuit. Fursona content is optional for everyone, including officers.
- Include ten FAQ items in a full-width, horizontal accordion. The first three established questions remain available as FAQ entries, with their simple answers also surfaced in the relevant page copy:
  1. Do I need a fursuit?
  2. I’m furry-adjacent or just curious. Can I come?
  3. How do I join?
- Add seven clearly identified sample questions and answers; Lorem ipsum is acceptable for these placeholders. Keep the questions visibly secondary to the three established club questions.
- Make joining through Discord easy to find. The actual invite URL is not yet available, so show a labeled placeholder rather than inventing one.

#### Events

Explain the monthly activity and direct visitors to Discord for the current date. Give this page its own event-poster or handbill composition.

- Feature **Howling at the Moon**: once a month, walk to Walking to the Sky, then get together for a movie and snacks.
- State that dates are announced on Discord. Do not invent an event date or promise pizza.
- Leave room for event photography in the slideshow or gallery area.
- Include a few sample recent-event recaps with date, title, and short excerpt. Label all dates and copy as sample content. Use unequal editorial notes or clippings rather than a repeated set of cards.

#### Officers

Help visitors meet the people who run the club.

- Show three placeholder officer profiles. Each profile needs a portrait/image area, name, role, pronouns, and a Lorem ipsum bio.
- Keep pronouns visible for every person. Use “Pronouns pending” where actual pronouns have not been supplied; do not guess.
- Give each person a related but individually composed profile. Vary portrait position, size, crop, and supporting marks while keeping the key information easy to locate.
- Fursona material is optional for each officer. Where included, it may be concept art, a character reference, or a photo of an actual fursuit. Do not imply that everyone has or wants one.
- Label all names, roles, biographies, and images as replaceable placeholders.

### Interaction and tone

- Keep interaction low-key and useful. The main actions are page navigation, joining Discord, and opening FAQ answers.
- FAQ entries should have a clear collapsed/expanded state. Links and controls should be apparent and usable without relying on hover.
- Small optional motion may support a printed mark or transition, but the design should not depend on animation. The photo slideshow is user-controlled and never autoplaying. Avoid sound, scroll hijacking, persistent bobbing, and promotional motion.
- Keep the copy plain, warm, and welcoming. Do not make the club sound like a product or invent specific policies, events, or promises beyond the details above.
